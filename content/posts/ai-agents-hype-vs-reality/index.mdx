I saw a tweet today.  

Someone claiming their AI agent “replaced a whole engineering team.”  

I rolled my eyes.  

Look, I’ve built software products for a decade. I’ve also spent the last year building AI agents for packaging compliance.  

Here’s the truth:  

AI agents are great at narrow, well-defined tasks. They fail hard at anything that requires real-world context, debugging, or human judgment.  

The tweet probably showed a demo. Demos are cherry-picked. They don’t show the edge cases, the broken API calls, the hallucinated output.  

**The real leverage isn’t the agent – it’s the workflow.**  

If you want to use AI agents practically:  

1. **Define the bottleneck precisely.** Not “automate everything” – just one repetitive decision.  
2. **Give it guardrails.** Hard-coded rules, human-in-the-loop for critical steps, logging every action.  
3. **Test in production for a week.** Not a demo. Real data, real errors.  

That’s how we built our compliance checker. It doesn’t replace people. It catches 80% of label errors before a human looks.  

The remaining 20%? That’s where experience lives.  

Don’t chase the demo. Build for the long-term.  

**TL;DR**  
- AI agent demos often mislead – real-world is messier  
- Focus on a single, well-scoped task, not full replacement  
- Add guardrails and test in production before trusting it  
- The workflow matters more than the model
