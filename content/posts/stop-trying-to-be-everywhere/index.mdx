I spent years trying to be everywhere.

Every social platform. Every industry event. Every new feature request from every customer.

All I got was burnout and a product that did 50 things poorly.

Then I watched this talk. It hit me like a brick.

**The problem isn't lack of ideas. It's lack of focus.**

Here's what I learned the hard way:

**1. Your attention is your only scarce resource.**

You can hire for talent. You can buy tools. You can borrow capital. But you cannot buy more hours in a day.

Every new channel you open means less energy for the one that actually works.

**2. Most things don't compound.**

When I look back at GoVisually's growth, 80% of our traction came from two things: product quality and word-of-mouth from packaging teams who actually used it.

Not Twitter threads. Not LinkedIn posts. Not conference booths.

The stuff that felt productive was often just noise.

**3. The real competitive advantage is depth.**

It's easy to be shallow in ten places. It's hard to be deep in one.

But deep is where the defensible moat lives. Deep is where customers tell their friends. Deep is where you can charge a premium because you actually solve the problem, not just scratch it.

**What I changed:**

- I stopped posting on platforms that didn't directly lead to conversations with packaging compliance teams.
- I started saying no to feature requests that didn't serve our core workflow.
- I killed half my meetings.

The result? More time for the work that matters. Better product. Happier customers.

**The takeaway:**

Before you add something new, ask yourself: "Will this compound? Or will it just keep me busy?"

Most things are just busy.

Choose the ones that build.

**TL;DR**

- Attention is scarce. Spend it where it compounds.
- Depth beats breadth for durable advantage.
- Say no to most things so you can say yes to the few that matter.
- Your product and your customers are the only channels that really compound.
