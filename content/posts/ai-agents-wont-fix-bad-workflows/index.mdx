Saw a tweet from @MichLieben: “If you automate a bad process, you get bad results faster.”  

That’s it. That’s the whole blog post.  

But let me unpack it because I see teams make this mistake all the time in CPG packaging compliance.  

They hear about AI agents. They think: “Great, we’ll throw an agent at our label review chaos. Problem solved.”  

Then they get faster chaos.  

Here’s a real example. A food brand came to us at GoVisually. They had 17 handoffs between design, legal, regulatory, and marketing. Every review required a new PDF, new comments, new emails.  

An AI agent reading those PDFs would just speed up the wrong workflow. It would still miss the fact that no one had a single source of truth for ingredient specs. The agent would be wrong faster.  

First principles question: What’s the bottleneck?  

It’s rarely the reading speed. It’s the workflow. Too many people touching the same file. No standardised inputs. Handoff delays.  

Fix that first.  

- Standardise your data inputs (ingredient lists, claims, allergens).  
- Reduce handoffs. One owner per review stage.  
- Use a shared system, not email threads.  

Then add AI agents.  

Now the agent works on a clean process. It checks real-time regulations against a known spec. It flags mismatches in seconds. The output is reliable because the input is clean.  

This isn’t anti-AI. It’s pro-execution.  

AI agents are great. But they amplify what’s already there. If “what’s there” is broken, you get broken results at machine speed.  

**TL;DR**  
- Automating a broken workflow just makes it faster and wronger.  
- Fix the process before adding AI: standardise inputs, reduce handoffs, use a shared system.  
- AI agents are a multiplier. Only apply them after you’ve cleaned up the bottleneck.
