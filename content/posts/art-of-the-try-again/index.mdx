I’ve lost count of how many times I’ve stared at a broken script, a rejected pitch, or a failed prototype and felt that familiar knot in my stomach. The first instinct is always the same: *This didn’t work. I must be doing something wrong.*

But here’s the shift that changed everything for me: **Failure isn’t a verdict. It’s data.**

## The Reframe

When you "try again," you aren’t repeating the same mistake. You’re running a new experiment. The first attempt gave you information—what *didn’t* work, what broke, where the bottleneck is. Now you have a better map.

Think of it like debugging code. You don’t give up when the app crashes; you read the error log and fix the bug. Life, work, and creativity work the same way.

## The Emotional Toll

Of course, the hardest part isn’t the logic—it’s the ego. Trying again means admitting you were wrong, or that your first effort wasn't enough. That stings.

But here’s a secret: **everyone who has ever built anything worthwhile has a graveyard of failed first attempts.** The difference is they kept the data and ditched the shame.

## Practical Tips for Trying Again

- **Wait 24 hours.** Let the emotional dust settle before you re-approach.
- **Write down one lesson.** What did this attempt teach you? Be specific.
- **Change one variable.** Don’t just do the same thing louder. Tweak the approach.
- **Lower the stakes.** The second try doesn’t have to be perfect—it just has to be better.

## TL;DR

- Failure is feedback, not a final judgment.
- Separate the emotional sting from the useful data.
- Wait, reflect, change one variable, then try again.
- The only real failure is stopping after the first "no."
