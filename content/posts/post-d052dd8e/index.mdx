# The Kimi K3 Lesson: Constraints Beat Hardware

Moonshot AI just published the largest parameter count ever for a model—2.8 trillion. Trained on weaker, export-restricted chips. Not Nvidia's best.

The market got spooked because if you can build a top model without the most advanced silicon, Nvidia's trillion-dollar narrative gets shaky. But here's what I actually took from it, as someone who's built products with limited resources.

## Key takeaways
- A 2.8 trillion parameter model trained on restricted hardware proves software optimization matters more than raw compute.
- For bootstrapped teams, this means you don't need the most expensive infrastructure to ship competitive AI products.
- Nvidia's moat is real, but not unassailable—efficiency gains reduce their pricing power over time.
- Constraints make you resourceful. Moonshot had to squeeze every drop from weaker chips. That forced innovation.

## What this actually means for founders

Most startup advice on AI assumes you need massive capital. Rent a cluster of H100s, spend millions on training runs, pray you find product-market fit before the money runs out.

Kimi K3 says: not necessarily.

Yes, they still spent heavily on training—Claire mentioned it's the priciest Chinese model release so far. But the interesting part is the *how*. Using weaker chips meant they had to optimise architecture, data quality, and training efficiency. They couldn't just throw compute at the problem.

That's exactly how bootstrappers think. You don't have the luxury of overprovisioning. You build lean, then you find the edges where small investments yield outsized returns.

## What I see in my own work

At GoVisually, we use AI for compliance checks on packaging labels. We're not training trillion-parameter models. We're taking existing models—some open-weight, some API-based—and applying them to a narrow, high-value problem. The hard part isn't the model size. It's the accuracy, the latency, the integration with customer workflows.

If I had waited until we could afford the best GPU setup, we'd still be waiting. Instead, we started with smaller models, iterated on the prompt engineering, built a feedback loop from real customers. That's what shipped a product.
