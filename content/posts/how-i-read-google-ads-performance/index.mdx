# How I Actually Read Google Ads Performance

Most advice on Google Ads is wrong. Here's how I look at mine as a bootstrapped founder.

**Tags:** Google Ads, PPC, bootstrapping, SaaS  
**Slug:** how-i-read-google-ads-performance

### Key takeaways

- Only two numbers matter: cost per qualified lead and close rate.
- Click-through rate is vanity. Quality Score is Google's game, not yours.
- Kill any campaign that doesn't generate profit within 30 days.
- Track conversions back to money, not just clicks.

## Start with profit, not performance

Open your Google Ads dashboard. What do you see first? CTR. Impressions. Quality Score. Maybe a big green graph showing clicks going up.

That's Google selling you on spending more.

I used to chase these numbers at Competeshark. Thought a 5% CTR meant something. Then I realized: those clicks didn't pay the bills. A high CTR can mean you're attracting tire-kickers. Or your targeting is too broad. Or people click accidentally on mobile.

The only question that matters: does this campaign make money?

If you can't answer that with real numbers — not "brand awareness" hand-waving — nothing else in the dashboard is useful.

## The two numbers I actually track

Cost per qualified lead. And close rate.

Multiply them: $50 cost per lead × 10% close rate = $500 CPA. Compare to your average customer lifetime value. If CPA is above LTV, pause the campaign. Full stop. No exceptions.

I don't track "cost per click" or "cost per thousand impressions." Those are intermediate numbers. What I need to know is: how much do I have to spend to get someone who will actually pay me?

That means defining "qualified lead" tightly. Not an email signup. Not a whitepaper download. A human who has demonstrated intent to buy — booked a demo, called my sales line, sent a detailed question about pricing.

[VERIFY: For GoVisually's packaging compliance product, a qualified lead is someone who filled out our contact form and mentioned FDA regulations. Cost per qualified lead currently runs around $85. Close rate is about 1 in 8. That gives us ~$680 CPA against $4,000+ LTV. It works.]

## What I ignore (and why)

**Click-through rate.** High CTR can mean cheap traffic from broad keywords. Or it can mean your ad is misleading. I've seen campaigns with 8% CTR that converted at 0%. I've seen 1.5% CTR campaigns that closed deals consistently. CTR correlates with relevance, not revenue.

**Quality Score.** This number exists so Google can justify charging you more for the same click. Higher quality score = lower CPC. Sure. But optimizing for Quality Score can lead you to write generic ads that match broad keywords. You end up paying less for worse traffic. Not a win.

**Impression share.** Means nothing if the impressions aren't converting.

**Average position.** Dead metric. Mobile vs desktop, extensions, competitor ads — position is fuzzy. Google doesn't even report it the same way anymore.

## When to shut down a campaign

Bootstrapped companies can't afford experiments that run for months. I give campaigns 30 days.

If in 30 days I haven't seen a single qualified lead that turned into a real conversation with a real human who might buy, the campaign is dead. Even if clicks look great. Even if CTR is 12%. Even if the account rep says "give it more time."

I've killed campaigns that had "great potential" because potential doesn't pay my engineers.

One example: at GoVisually we ran two campaigns side by side. One targeting "creative review software" — high CTR, low cost. The other targeting "FDA label compliance" — expensive, low CTR. Guess which one produced actual revenue? The compliance campaign. The creative review campaign looked better in the dashboard but closed zero deals in 60 days. I killed it after 30. Should have killed it sooner.
