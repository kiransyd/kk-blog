I watched a 2 hour video on Cloudflare’s developer day about AI agents.

It was worth every minute.

Not because of the hype. Because of the practical decisions they made.

Here’s what I took away.

## The bottleneck is workflow, not models

Most people think AI agents are about picking the right model.

Cloudflare showed the opposite.

The hard part is building a system that reliably calls APIs, handles errors, and retries when things break. The model is just one component.

They built a “agent” that does exactly that. It’s not magic. It’s good engineering.

## Self-hosting matters more than you think

They run their own inference on GPUs.

Why? Control and latency.

When you depend on third-party APIs for every request, your product breaks when they throttle you or change pricing. Self-hosting gives you a durable advantage.

It’s not for everyone. But if you’re building something that needs to be fast and reliable, it’s worth the trade-off.

## The real use case is boring

The demo was about automating a support ticket workflow.

Not generating poetry. Not creating art. Just routing a customer complaint to the right team and suggesting a response.

That’s where AI agents actually work. In boring, repetitive workflows where the cost of a mistake is low and the value of speed is high.

## What I’d do differently

Cloudflare’s approach is impressive. But it’s also complex.

For most startups, I’d start with a simple wrapper around an API. Get the workflow right first. Then optimize for latency and cost.

Don’t build a self-hosted inference pipeline until you have 1000+ users asking for it.

## TL;DR

- AI agents are about systems, not models
- Self-hosting gives you control but adds complexity
- The best use cases are boring, repetitive workflows
- Start simple, optimize later
