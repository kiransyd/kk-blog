**TITLE:** The real reason AI agents haven't taken over yet

**DESCRIPTION:** It's not about the models being dumb. It's about the boring, hard stuff nobody wants to build.

**TAGS:** AI agents, SaaS, product design, practical AI

**SLUG:** ai-agents-arent-taking-over-yet

I saw a tweet from Michiel Lieben the other day. He nailed it.

The big AI models? They're getting smarter every week. The bottleneck isn't intelligence anymore.

It's the boring stuff.

The plumbing. The orchestration. The reliability.

Here's what I mean.

An LLM can write a perfect email. But can it reliably fetch the right customer data from your CRM, check your inventory system, validate the shipping address, and then *not* hallucinate the tracking number?

That's not a model problem. That's a systems problem.

Every AI agent needs:

- Authentication with 10 different APIs
- Error handling when one of them is down
- A way to recover from a mistake without breaking the whole workflow
- Logging so you can debug why it sent the wrong response

Nobody is building that. Everyone is building the flashy demo.

I've seen this pattern before. In the early days of SaaS, everyone wanted to build the frontend. The backend was an afterthought. Then companies like Stripe and Twilio showed up and made the plumbing valuable.

Same thing is happening now.

The real durable advantage in AI agents won't come from the model. It'll come from the infrastructure that makes them actually work in production.

**TL;DR:**
- AI models are improving fast, but agents still fail in real-world use
- The bottleneck is not intelligence, it's reliability and orchestration
- Most teams focus on the flashy demo, not the boring infrastructure
- The durable advantage is in the plumbing, not the model
