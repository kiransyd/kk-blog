# How TikTok’s Tagline Became My Operating System

I saw the source link and thought: that’s the whole post right there.

“TikTok – Make Your Day.”

Two things hit me. First, I wasted 30 seconds waiting for content that didn’t exist. Second, the tagline is actually good. It’s not about consuming. It’s about *making*.

That’s how I run GoVisually. Not hoping the market hands me a good day. Building it.

### “Make” is the verb too few founders use

Most startup advice is passive. “Find product-market fit.” “Wait for the right moment.” “Let the data tell you.”

I’ve built four products now. Two died. Two survived. The difference wasn’t timing. It was me deciding to *make* something happen.

When Competeshark and Pricify failed, I didn’t wait for a signal. I made calls. Built again. When GoVisually’s original use case (creative review) hit a ceiling, I didn’t wait for a pivot to emerge. I started calling packaging compliance teams, asking where their pain was.

That’s how I found the CPG compliance angle. By making the conversation happen.

### The trap of the algorithm

TikTok gives you what you’ve already liked. Your feed becomes a mirror.

Business advice does the same. LinkedIn algorithms push content that confirms your bias. Bootstrapper? Here’s ten stories about why VC is evil. Funded? Here’s ten stories about why bootstrapping is a trap.

I’ve had both. And the pattern is: the people who succeed are the ones who stop consuming the algorithm’s mirror and start making their own decisions.

For me that meant ignoring 90% of SaaS growth playbooks. They’re written for companies with $10M in funding and a growth team. I have neither. So I built direct sales conversations into my calendar. I write emails to customers myself. I deploy features same-day when a customer asks for them.

That’s making my day. Not reading about how to do it.

### Practical takeaway

Start tomorrow with a simple rule:

Before you consume any business content (blog, podcast, LinkedIn post), ask: “Am I about to consume, or am I about to *make*?”

If it’s consumption, spend 10 minutes. Then close the tab and do something that changes a metric.

Send one email. Ship one line of code. Call one customer.

That’s how you make your day.
